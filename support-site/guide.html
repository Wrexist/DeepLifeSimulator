How Deep Life works

Deep Life Simulator is a free life simulation game for iPhone, iPad and Android. This page explains how a life plays out, how money works, and what to do in your first weeks.

What kind of game is Deep Life Simulator?

It is a text-first life simulator with a real economy underneath. You start adult life with next to nothing and decide how to spend each week: work, study, hustle, invest, date, rest, or take a risk. Every choice moves your money, health, energy, happiness and relationships, and the effects add up over decades.

When your character dies, the life is not over for the family. Your heir takes over what you built, and the next generation starts from there.

How does a turn work?

One tap moves time forward one week. A year is 52 weeks. Each week your salary is paid, bills and rent are charged, markets move, relationships drift, and life events can happen. Because time moves in weeks, short-term decisions matter: a missed bill turns into arrears, and a week of rest shows up in your energy.

How do you make money?

  • Careers. About 30 careers with promotion ladders, from entry-level work up to senior roles, and a political career with elections and office.
  • Street work and side hustles. Quick cash when you are starting out.
  • Education. Degrees cost money and time but open better-paid careers.
  • Stocks and crypto. A market that moves every week. Spreading money across holdings is steadier than betting on one.
  • Real estate. Rent a home, buy property, collect rent from tenants.
  • Companies. Found a business, hire staff, and grow it.
  • Crime and the dark web. Faster money with real risk attached.

Loans are available, and their interest is real, so borrowing to invest can make you rich or break you.

What happens when you die?

You get an obituary of the life you lived, which you can share. Then your heir inherits the fortune, the family name and whatever mess you left. Lives also earn legacy points, which buy permanent advantages for the next generation, and prestige tiers unlock dynasty systems as the family grows.

What are Mindsets?

At the start of a life you pick a Mindset. Each one shapes the whole life with one clear upside and one downside, for example faster promotions, cheaper property, or better luck on the street, each with a cost elsewhere. The numbers on the card are exactly the numbers the game applies. Heirs inherit their parent's Mindset.

Tips for your first life

  • Get a job in week one. Steady pay keeps bills from turning into debt.
  • Eat, but not too much. The first three meals each week restore full energy. After that, food helps less.
  • Watch your bills. Bills you cannot pay become arrears, which follow you until you clear them.
  • Keep a few people close. Close relationships lift your happiness each week, and people you ignore drift away.
  • Happiness has diminishing returns. Gains are worth less the happier you already are, so fix what is low first.
  • Spread your investments. One stock can crash. A spread of holdings grows more steadily over years.

Is Deep Life Simulator like BitLife?

Yes, in spirit. Both are choice-driven life sims you play on your phone. If you like BitLife, the main differences are how time moves and how deep the money goes.

Deep Life Simulator
TimeOne week per tap, 52 weeks a year
MoneyWeekly salary, bills, rent, loans with interest, arrears
InvestingA stock market and crypto that move every week, plus property and companies
After deathYour heir inherits the fortune and family name; legacy points and dynasty tiers carry across generations
PriceFree and playable offline. Full-screen ads only at year-end breaks, none in the first two in-game years; optional DeepLife+ removes them

BitLife is the best-known game in the genre, plays a year per tap and has a huge library of events and challenges. Deep Life is the one to try if you want the economy to matter more.
