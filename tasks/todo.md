# Active plan — bring the income side back down and make it consistent

Owner report (2026-08-04): "man får för mycket pengar… nu är spelet för enkelt",
with a save showing $50K at age 21. Confirmed mine, and measured.

Owner decision: **lower salaries, lower street jobs, then balance everything to
that.** Not a cost-of-living layer.

---

## What I actually did wrong

1. **Salary rescale overshot.** `MIN_ENTRY_WEEKLY_SALARY = 350` was 7x the old
   bottom rung. Measured on the real career data: 3 years on fast food yields
   $52 460, which matches the owner's screenshot almost exactly. Time to afford
   the $95 000 studio went from 37 years to 6.

2. **The inflation indexing is a bug, not a balance choice.** I tied career pay
   to `priceIndex` and wrote — in the code and in my summary — that this made
   inflation "neutral in real terms". It does not. `getInflatedPrice` reaches
   exactly two production surfaces: company founding and mining upgrades.
   Property, vehicles, luxury, food, items and education all carry fixed
   catalogue prices. So pay compounds +3%/yr against prices that never move:
   x1.35 at 10 years, x6.05 at 60. That is what turns the 40-year figure from
   7x into 13x. I verified the salary side and never checked the price side.

## What was already out of scale, and has to move with it

3. **Street jobs pay more than a career.** Energy regen is 40/week and a career
   costs 5, so a player can hold a job AND grind. Optimal street play is
   ~$250-380/wk against a $120-350/wk career — so the "career is better than
   grinding" intent never held at any salary level I could pick.

4. **Rent yields 26%/yr.** `RENT_INCOME_RATE = 0.005`/wk means a $95 000 studio
   pays $475/wk and repays itself in under four years. That is the real money
   printer, and lowering wages without touching it just makes property the only
   sane strategy.

---

## Plan

- [x] Remove the `priceIndex` factor from `applyCareerSalaryAndPenalty`. Inflation
      keeps running (it is a real system again) but it must not move wages while
      the prices it reaches are two screens deep.
- [x] `MIN_ENTRY_WEEKLY_SALARY` 350 -> 120, ladders regenerated by the same
      documented rule. Only the genuinely absurd ladders lift; teacher/nurse and
      the whole professional tier stay untouched.
- [x] Scale street-job `basePayment` so an energy-optimal week lands BELOW a
      comparable career, since street work also costs health and happiness and
      carries arrest risk.
- [x] `RENT_INCOME_RATE` down to a realistic gross yield. Note it is shared with
      what the player PAYS in rent, so the change cuts both ways — that is
      correct, but it has to be stated rather than discovered.
- [x] A balance model script so the numbers are measured, re-runnable and
      reviewable instead of asserted in a commit message.
- [x] Regression test pinning the relationships: a career beats grinding, rent
      does not repay a property in a handful of years, and no ladder starts
      below the floor.

## Target, stated up front so the result can be judged against it

| | Target |
|---|---|
| Studio ($95k) on the bottom rung, no expenses | ~15-20 years |
| Same after climbing that ladder | ~5-7 years |
| Street grinding vs holding a career | strictly worse per week |
| Rent gross yield | high-single-digit %/yr, not 26 |

---

## Result, measured (`npx jest incomeScale` prints this)

|  | Before the report | After |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom-rung career | $350/wk | **$110/wk** |
| Best street week (expected) | $234/wk | **$95/wk** |
| Street vs career | 0.67x | **0.86x**, and now below a career at every rung |
| Studio on the bottom rung | 5.5 years | **16.6 years** |
| Studio at the top of that ladder | 3.0 years | **8.0 years** |
| Rent gross yield | 26.0%/yr | **7.8%/yr** |
| Property repays itself in | 3.8 years | **12.8 years** |

Three years on the bottom rung — the owner's screenshot — goes from **$52,460 to
about $17,000**.

Two things worth flagging rather than burying:

- Lowering `RENT_INCOME_RATE` also makes RENTING a home cheaper, because one
  constant drives both directions. The landlord side was the exploit, so the
  trade is right, but it is a real second-order effect.
- There is still no baseline cost of living, so a player with no property and no
  diet plan pays only income tax (~4%, and nothing at all under $200/wk). Income
  is now small enough that this is much less visible, but the money axis still
  has no recurring outgoing. That remains the honest next lever if it still
  reads as too easy — and it is a design decision, not a fix.
