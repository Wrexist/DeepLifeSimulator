# 03 — Negative keywords

On a $30/day account this is the highest-ROI file in the folder. Negative
keywords do not win you auctions; they stop you paying for auctions you should
never have entered. With Search Match on in Discovery, an unguarded account
finds those auctions within days.

| File | Rows | Apply at | Apply to |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`negatives/global-negatives.csv`](negatives/global-negatives.csv) | 169 | Campaign level | Category, Competitor, Discovery, MaxConv — **not** Brand |
| [`negatives/discovery-graduated.csv`](negatives/discovery-graduated.csv) | 126 | Campaign level | Discovery only — regenerated by script |
| [`negatives/adgroup-crosslocks.csv`](negatives/adgroup-crosslocks.csv) | 28 | Ad group level | The 8 Category ad groups |

**Every negative is EXACT match.** A broad negative blocks the keyword *and*
everything containing it — one broad `money` negative silently mutes the entire
`Money-Wealth` ad group, and the symptom (impressions quietly stop) looks nothing
like the cause. Apple supports broad negatives; on a keyword set this
thematically tight there is no case for them.

---

## Why this app needs an unusually large block list

Three of the app's core nouns are among the most polluted tokens in App Store
search. This is not generic hygiene — it is specific to the words this game owns.

### "life" → insurance, family locators, and half a dozen unrelated meanings

`life insurance` and its variants are among the highest-commercial-intent
searches on any platform, and `life360` is a top-charts family-locator app. Both
sit directly on top of your head term. Also blocked: `half life`, `life is
strange`, `still life`, `shelf life`, `wildlife`, `sea life`, `nightlife`,
`afterlife`, `life coach`, `life planner`, `life expectancy calculator`. Every
one of those is a different product category that a broad match on "life" will
find.

### "simulator" → the single largest genre trap in mobile

Vehicle and animal simulators dominate the token: truck, bus, flight, farming,
train, car, driving, parking, drift, forklift, tractor, snow plow, lawn mowing,
power wash, plane, ship, cooking, fishing, surgeon, police, army, war, goat, and
the whole animal-sim family (cat, dog, horse, wolf, lion, tiger, shark, spider,
dinosaur, bee, ant). These convert *well* — for the wrong app. A user searching
"truck simulator" who taps your ad and bounces costs you a full CPT and teaches
Apple's relevance model that your ad is a poor answer.

### money / stocks / real estate / career / dating → real-utility apps

Every economic system in this game has a real-world app category sitting on its
keyword: `cash app`, `paypal`, `budget app`, `loan app`, `credit score`;
`robinhood`, `coinbase`, `binance`, `tradingview`, `stock tracker`; `zillow`,
`redfin`, `mortgage calculator`; `indeed`, `linkedin`, `resume builder`;
`tinder`, `bumble`. Someone searching "stock tracker" wants a portfolio tool and
will never install a game. This is where Discovery's Search Match will drift
first, because your product page genuinely does mention stocks, loans, careers,
and dating.

### Two blocks worth calling out separately

- **Piracy and cheat intent** — `mod apk`, `mod menu`, `unlimited money mod`,
  `hack`, `cheats`, `cracked`, `unblocked`, `apk`. These never become paying
  users, and they are disproportionately likely to leave a bad review when the
  app declines to be modded. On a 2.3★ page that is an active liability, not just
  wasted spend.
- **Kids terms** — `kids games`, `games for kids`, `toddler games`,
  `baby games`, `preschool games`. The game ships a crime and dark-web system.
  A parent who installs it for a child is a refund, a one-star review, and an
  age-rating complaint. Block these before you spend anything.

## What is deliberately *not* blocked

- **`free`** — mobile-game searchers append "free" constantly and the app is
  free-to-play with optional DeepLife+. Blocking it would cut real demand.
- **`second life`, `the sims mobile`, `avakin life`, `sims freeplay`** — these
  are competitors, not noise. They belong in
  [`keywords/competitor-exact.csv`](keywords/competitor-exact.csv) where you can
  bid on them deliberately, or nowhere. Never in a global negative list: a
  negative in a shared list would also block them in the Competitor campaign.
- **Anything you have not seen in a search-term report.** Speculative negatives
  beyond the categories above cost you reach for a saving you cannot measure. The
  169 rows here are all traps this app's specific vocabulary guarantees; go
  further only with report evidence.

---

## Brand campaign gets no negatives

Deliberate. Every search that reaches an exact brand keyword is someone typing
your app's name — there is nothing to filter, and a negative list applied there
can only ever cost you a branded install. If a global list is applied to Brand by
accident, `life insurance` and `deep life` interact in ways you will spend an
afternoon diagnosing.

## The weekly negative-mining routine

15 minutes, every Monday, before anything else in `06`:

1. Apple Ads → `DLS-US-Discovery-Broad` → **Search Terms** report, last 7 days.
2. Sort by **spend**, descending.
3. For every term with spend and **zero installs**:
   - Irrelevant (wrong genre, wrong vertical, wrong platform, cheat/mod intent)
     → add as an EXACT negative at **campaign** level and append it to
     `negatives/global-negatives.csv` with a `block_reason`.
   - Relevant but not converting → leave it one more week. A single week at
     $30/day is not enough data to condemn a relevant term; two is.
4. For every term with **≥1 install at or below target CPA** → graduate it
   (`02-keywords.md`), then `node marketing/apple-ads/build-negatives.js`.
5. Commit the changed CSVs. The block list is an asset that compounds — the
   version in git is what makes month 6 cheaper than month 1, and what lets you
   rebuild the account from scratch or clone it into a new country in an hour.

**Red flags that mean the list has gone stale:** CPA climbing while CPT is flat
(you are buying more of the wrong traffic), Discovery spend rising as a share of
account spend without new graduations, or your exact campaigns' CPT drifting up
with no competitive change — that last one usually means Discovery is bidding
against them because a graduation skipped step 4.
